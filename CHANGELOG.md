## [1.4.2] - 2026-10-21

### Fixed
- **A totem kept for a player arrives the way a transferred totem does** (BSP-InventorySync request R5). Since logins wait for the inventory restore and for a respawn, a Shatter Totem collected from the shared database for a player who is still waiting, or dead, is kept on the server and handed over later. It was then handed over like any other item: in a full inventory it could be dropped on the ground, and its arrival was not written in the totem ledger, so a totem reissued on another server could be spent as a stale copy. Kept totems (and totems in any delivery to a player online) now go through the same hand-back as a transfer: the ledger records the arrival, the player's departure record is cleared, and the totem goes into the selected or first free slot, moving another stack aside if it must, never dropped. Other owed items are handed over as before. Two game tests.

## [1.4.1] - 2026-10-21

### Added
- **Totem calls for inventory sync mods** (BSP-InventorySync requests R1 and R2). `BspTotems.isTotem(ItemStack)` says whether a stack is a live Shatter Totem (not a spent husk), so a snapshot can leave every live totem out; `BspTotems.giveBack(ServerPlayer, ItemStack)` hands a totem back into the selected or the first free slot and never drops the totem, so a restore can return the totems it finds through BSP-Core. Both use the rules BSP-Core already applies to every inventory. Two game tests.

### Changed
- **Logins wait for BSP-InventorySync's restore** (its request R3). With that mod installed, the part of a login that touches the inventory and the player's saved data waits for its `InventoryRestoredEvent`: Shatter Coins from an older epoch are stripped, the season check runs (last season's totems out, a fresh one in) and the items owed to the player are handed over only once the restored inventory is in place, so a restore can no longer overwrite a delivery or bring old coins back. While a player waits, the items waiting for them in the shared database stay there. The prize list is still sent at once. Config `storage.deliverOnRestore` (true) and `storage.restoreWaitSeconds` (30: after it the login settles anyway, with a line in the log). Without the mod, logins work as before. Two game tests.
- The compile-time dependency on BSP-Region Guard reads its 0.3.0 jar (the 0.2.0 jar is gone from its checkout; its API is unchanged at version 5); nothing BSP-Core calls changed.

### Fixed
- **Nothing is handed to a player on the death screen** (BSP-InventorySync request R4). A player who logged out dead logs back in dead, and a dead player's inventory does not pass to the respawned one unless keepInventory is on, so coins, owed items and handed-back Shatter Totems given at that moment were lost. A login is never settled on a dead player: without BSP-InventorySync it is settled on the respawned player at the respawn; with it, the `storage.restoreWaitSeconds` countdown stands still while the player is dead and starts again from the respawn, a player still waiting at the respawn keeps waiting for the restore (which comes after it), and every settle goes to the player's current entity. Items that come for a player while they are dead (a delivery, the shared database's regular collection) wait and are handed over at the respawn. Three game tests.
- **Kits and the logout totem wait for the respawn too.** A kit owed to a player on the death screen (a first-join kit, a reward, a gift) stays owed and is delivered to the respawned player, or by BSP-InventorySync's restore when the login still waits for it; a Shatter Totem placed at the player's logout stays standing while they log back in dead and comes back to them at the respawn. Two game tests.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-10-19

### Added
- **Kits, part 1: the kit and its rules.** A kit is an admin-made bundle of up to `kit.maxItems` (36) stacks with their full NBT, a name, a line of description and an icon, with rules: shown in the players' browser or not, once-only or a cooldown, a price in Shatter Pieces, LuckPerms ranks that may take it, servers it may be taken on, and how it is handed out by itself (never, at the first login on the network, or at the first login on listed servers). What a kit is to a player is one state at a time, checked in order: owed, hidden, wrong server, rank, already taken, on cooldown, too dear, no room, ready. A kit is never put into an inventory that cannot take it: the space is worked out on a copy of the 36 main slots first (part stacks topped up, armour and offhand never used), the price is charged only after that, and a reward or gift that does not fit waits as owed and is delivered when it does. Records are saved with the world (`bsp_core_kits`); the shared tables follow in part 6, the screens in parts 3 and 4, the first-login grants in part 5, the LuckPerms bridge in part 7. Admin commands `/bsp kit list | info | give | reset | delete | test` (kits are made in the panel's KITS tab, part 3). `BspKits` and `KitEvent.Given` / `KitEvent.Owed` in the api for RankUp and the other mods. Config `[kit]`. Four game tests.
- **Kits, part 3: the KITS tab on the admin panel and the kit editor.** `/bsp admin` has a fourth tab, KITS (`/bsp admin kits` opens the panel on it): the kits in a list with their icon, the chosen kit's summary beside it (its first eighteen items, how it is given, the take rule, the browser flag, ranks, servers, price, how often it was taken and by whom last, and a count of items this server cannot give), and the keys NEW KIT, EDIT, GIVE TO… (the players online here as chips; the kit becomes owed to the one clicked and lands at once when it fits) and DELETE (SURE? within three seconds). EDIT or NEW KIT turns the panel into the editor, 344 x 310: the kit's 36 slots top left (click takes a slot out, the wheel sets its count, with shift by eight), FROM HAND copies the held stack with its NBT and FROM INVENTORY replaces the contents with the admin's main inventory; the item browser top right with a search line, chips per mod (all, minecraft, bsp_core, then every other namespace four at a time), a wheel-scrolled grid (click adds one, shift-click a full stack, hover names the item) and the settings rows under both: Name (a new kit's id comes from it), Icon (first slot or pick an item), Describe, Given (never, first join on the network, first join on servers… with a Grant on row of server chips), Browser shown or hidden, Take once or every N min / h / d, Price in pieces, Servers (everywhere or chips, + types another id), Ranks (anyone, the known group names as chips, + types one; when RankUp's ladder is known the row is the ladder drawn as steps, click a step for that rank and up), Enabled; keys SAVE, TEST ON ME (saves, then gives the admin the kit with no record), DELETE, CLOSE. A note at the bottom right says what the last action did; a refusal's reason goes to chat. Moderators see the tab read-only. One game test (the packets and the edit path).
- **Kits, part 4: the players' kit browser.** A KITS button beside TEAM above the inventory (`kitsButton` in the client config hides it), a key binding in Controls (unbound to start) and `/kits` open a wall of cards, four a row, one per kit the player may see. Every card carries the kit's icon and name and what it is to the player right now, decided by the server: a ready card has REDEEM on it; a kit waiting for them (a reward or gift that did not fit, or came while they were away) is violet with TAKE IT and the slots it needs; a locked card shows the rank to reach (as a step on RankUp's ladder with the player's own step marked, once the ladder is known), the server to join, the price with a bar of what they have, or the slots to free; a redeemed kit sits greyed with the time until it is ready again, or "once only". Clicking a card's icon flips it over the wall: the items with their counts, the description and the rule, and the "to get it" lines ticked or crossed (rank, price, free slots, server), with the card's key and BACK. The header names the player, their rank when known, their pieces and free slots; the wall refreshes every two seconds and scrolls with the wheel. Kits kept out of the browser appear only while they are waiting for the player. One game test.
- **Kits, part 5: first-join kits and delivery after login.** At every login, each enabled kit set to be given at the first join that the player has no record of becomes owed to them: a kit set to the **network** once for the whole network (it then counts as taken, so a once-only kit is done), a kit set to **servers** once per server that hands it out (every server when none is listed). A player who joined before the kit existed gets it on their next login, like everyone without a record (`/bsp kit reset` makes them new again). Nothing is put into the inventory at login time itself: what is owed is delivered once the inventory is settled, on BSP-InventorySync's restore event when that mod is installed (`kit.deliverOnRestore`, on), else `kit.grantDelayTicks` (40) after the login. A kit that does not fit stays waiting on its card with a line in chat saying how many slots to free. One game test.
- **Kits, part 6: one set of kits across the network.** With `storage.mode = "mysql"` kits live in three shared tables, `kits`, `kit_items` (one row per stack, the NBT as text) and `kit_redeems` (one row per player, kit and scope: the network-wide row the once rule, the cooldown and the owed count live on, and a row per server for a per-server first-join grant). A kit saved or deleted on one server is announced over Redis (kind `kit`) and every other server puts the fresh copy in place of its own; a record a server writes (a redeem, a grant, a reward owed or delivered) reaches the others the same way, and a player's rows are read from the database at every login before anything is granted, so a reward given while they were away on another server waits for them here, a kit taken on one server is taken everywhere, and a once-only or cooldown kit cannot be taken twice by hopping servers. A network-wide first-join kit is claimed with an insert that only one server can win. The admin panel's server chips list every server the network has seen. An item from a mod a server lacks is dropped from that server's copy and counted ("N items this server cannot give" in the panel). `/bsp storage migrate` copies the local kits and records up. Without network storage the kits stay a record of the one server, as before. One game test (copies read from the database replace the local kit and rows; item rows round-trip with NBT; the SQL itself waits for the MySQL live test).
- **Kits, part 7: ranks through LuckPerms, and RankUp's ladder.** A kit's Ranks are LuckPerms group names, read through the same bridge BSP-Region Guard and BSP-RankUp use: the Forge edition directly, or the Bukkit plugin on a Mohist server through its plugin manager. A player in any of the kit's groups, directly or by inheritance, may take it; with LuckPerms out of reach a ranked kit stays locked and the log says so once. The admin panel's Ranks row offers the known groups, and when BSP-RankUp is installed and has a ladder it becomes that ladder drawn as rising steps (click a step for that rank and everything above it); the players' cards show the ladder with their own step marked and how many ranks are left to the one they need. Nothing is a compile-time dependency: LuckPerms' api is compile-only and RankUp is read by name. One game test.
- **Kits, part 8: the guide.** Guide book entry "Kits" in the coins category (opening the browser, reading a card, kits waiting for you, looking inside).
- **Other mods' boards on the Score Screen** (BSP-Teleport request R8). A mod registers a board on the client with `BspBoards.register(id, title, lines)`, each line a text, an optional value, an optional item icon and a colour. A screen's settings gain **Board** (show one registered board) and **Rotate** (turn through pages), a row to pick the board, a **Board look** (Ledger: rows with icon, text and a gold value; Tiles: the icon over the value in large type; Side column: the leaderboard keeps two thirds and the boards turn in a column at the right), the pages **In rotation** (the scoreboard, the rules, the prizes and every registered board; the scoreboard and the boards are in by default) and the seconds **Every** page stays (10; 5 to 120). A rotating screen fades each page in and shows a row of dots under the title, the current page lit; a board's page carries the board's own title. A board a client does not have says so once and is then left out of that client's rotation. Long boards scroll at the screen's speed. One game test.
- **`/bsp totem wipe <player>`**: a clean slate for one player, for testing accounts and support. Their carried totems are taken, their placed totems removed from the world, every ledger record of their totems forgotten (held, dropped, in transit), their logout placements, pending transfer and owed items dropped, the first-join grant cleared, and a fresh Tier I totem handed to them as on a first join. The admin sees what was done. One game test.

### Fixed
- **No more "someone took it" for a totem nobody took.** The logout-return rule reported every placement that no longer stood as taken, including totems the owner had destroyed in creative, picked up themselves, or that had been reissued or forgotten. It now says so only when the ledger shows that totem with someone else (a completed steal); every other case is silent. One game test.
### Changed
- The compile-time dependency on BSP-Region Guard reads its 0.2.0 jar (the 0.1.0 jar is gone from its checkout); nothing BSP-Core calls changed.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-10-18

### Added
- **Teams, part 1: the team itself.** Players can belong to one team of up to `team.maxMembers` (5): a leader and members, a name of `team.nameMin` to `team.nameMax` characters (3 to 16; letters, digits and spaces; unique on the server, whatever the case) and a chat colour. An invite waits `team.inviteSeconds` (60) for an answer and holds a seat while it does; every member may invite unless `team.membersInvite` is off. A member leaves when they like; the leader hands the lead over or disbands first. The leader removes members, renames, recolours and pins one note for the team; the team keeps the last `team.historySize` (50) lines left for it, each member knowing which are new to them. The record is saved with the world (`bsp_core_teams`). Admins have `/bsp team list | info <team> | disband <team> | kick <player> | rename <team> <name> | lead <team> <player>`. Nothing in the game uses a team yet: the shared totem access, the Team screen, the invite popup and the chat follow in the next parts. Four game tests.
- `BspTeams` in the api (`teamOf`, `team`, `sameTeam`, `teammates`, `all`) and `TeamEvent` (`Created`, `Joined`, `Left` with its reason, `LeaderChanged`, `Disbanded`) for the other mods.

### Fixed
- **Other mods' keys no longer act inside BSP-Core's screens.** Typing a letter that another mod had bound (G, say) while the Team screen or another of this mod's screens was open closed or replaced the screen, because the key was offered to every mod before and after the screen. A key pressed in a BSP-Core screen now goes to that screen and no further; Escape still closes as before.
- **Teams, part 2: access through the team.** A teammate counts as a friend with every flag on each of their teammates' placed totems: they open its panel, buy upgrades and raise its tier (paying as the owner would, with their own coins and XP), change its chunks, open its plasma machines and projectors, see through its cloak, and its Ward and Alarm leave them alone. The per-totem ACCESS list stays for people outside the team. Teammates cannot start a steal on each other's totems (`team.teammatesSteal` false) and the Totem Compass does not point at a teammate's totem (`team.compassIgnoresTeam` true). Leaving, being removed or a disband takes the access away at once, and a totem stolen by an outsider is out of the team's reach by itself. The panel now shows its buy keys and the CHUNKS tab to anyone the server would let buy, which also lets a friend with the Upgrades flag buy from the panel (the server accepted them before; the screen did not). One game test.
- **Teams, part 3: the Team screen.** A violet-framed board with keys along the bottom: MEMBERS (the team's name in its colour, each member with a dot for online here or offline with when they were last seen, the leader marked, how many totems they hold), INVITE (players online here and in no team, one key each; an invite you sent shows how long it has left to be answered), MESSAGES (the board: the leader's pinned note on top, the last fifty lines newest at the bottom with a green mark on the ones new to you, a line to type and SEND or Enter; a count of new lines sits on the key until you open it), TEAM CHAT (switches your chat to the team until pressed again; the chat itself follows in part 5), and for the leader SETTINGS (rename, recolour from sixteen swatches, HAND OVER or REMOVE each member, PIN or take down the note) and DISBAND; a member has LEAVE. Keys that cannot be undone ask SURE? and act on the second click within three seconds. With no team the screen is the maker's: a name, the swatches and CREATE. It opens from the new TEAM tab on your own or a teammate's placed totem, a TEAM tab in the header of your held totem's screen, a TEAM button above the inventory (so a player with no key bound and no totem in reach still gets in; `teamButton` in the client config hides it), or a key you bind in Controls (unbound to start). The screen refreshes itself every two seconds. One game test.
- **Teams, part 4: the invite card.** An invite now arrives three ways at once: a card at the top right of the screen, where the game's own toasts appear, with the inviter's face, their name and the team's name in its colour, how many members it has and who leads it, the two keys and the seconds left, with a line along its bottom edge draining as the minute runs out; a quiet chime; and a chat line "X invited you to join Y" with clickable Accept and Decline, which is how a player inside an inventory or another screen answers. The keys are the team prompts' own, Y to accept and H to decline (Controls > BSP-Core), apart from the Recall offer's R and N. Several cards stack downward, the top one bright and answering the keys, the ones waiting dimmed. A card leaves when answered from anywhere, when its minute is up, or when the team is disbanded. The inviter is told when the invite is accepted, declined or runs out. One game test.
- **Teams, part 5: team chat and the board.** With TEAM CHAT on (the key on the Team screen), what a member types goes to the team instead of to everyone: it lands on the team's board and reaches the members online as "[Team] name: text" in the team's colour, and a "TEAM CHAT" pill in that colour sits above the chat lines until they switch back (it also names the team while the chat box is open). `/t <message>` sends one line to the team without switching. Members who were offline find the lines on the board; at login they hear how many are new and see the leader's pinned note once. Leaving, being removed or a disband switches the channel back. With `team.chatTag` on, a member's lines to everyone carry the team's name in its colour (off by default, so a permissions mod's prefixes stay as they are). One game test.
- **Teams, part 6: one team across the network.** With `storage.mode = "mysql"` teams live in three shared tables, `teams`, `team_members` and `team_messages` (with the usual prefix): every change a server makes is written there and announced over Redis (kind `team`), and the other servers read the team back and put it in place of their copy, so a member who joins, leaves or is removed on one server is in or out everywhere within a moment, their totems' access follows, a line posted on one server reaches the members online on the others as it is posted, and a member's board, unread count and pinned note are the same wherever they log in. The member list says "on server-2" for a teammate online on another server of the network (each server marks its players in and out, and clears its marks when it starts). Each server loads every team at start, so names are unique across the network. Without network storage the team stays a record of the one server, as before. One game test (a copy read from the database replaces the local team: a member who joined elsewhere, a line posted elsewhere, a rename, a leaving, a disband).
- **Teams, part 7: the guide.** Guide book entry "Teams" in the totem category beside "Letting Friends In" (what a team gives, making one, the invite card, members and the lead, talking and the board, upgrading together), and a line in "Letting Friends In" pointing to it.
- **Team teleports, BSP-Core's side.** Once a teleport mod has declared itself (`BspTeams.teleportsHandledBy`), every online teammate's row in the Team screen's MEMBERS view carries a TELEPORT key; pressing it posts `TeamEvent.TeleportRequest` (the player, the teammate, and the network server the teammate is on), and the teleport mod moves the player under its own rules: no price for a team teleport, but its cooldown and warm-up, the destination's access and zone flags, and its raid rule, which refuses a landing within 150 blocks of any running steal on the network. Not to yourself, not to a teammate offline everywhere, never to an outsider; with no teleport mod the key is not drawn and a request says so (BSP-Teleport answers every request it receives, refusals included, so that line only ever shows in a pack without it). For the bracelet's team tab: `BspTeams.members(server, teamId)` with each member's online state and server. One game test.
- **A chat relay for the Velocity proxy** (BSP-Velocity Additions, request VA-C2). With `proxy.relayChat = true` (off by default) every public chat line that passed this server's listeners is sent to the proxy as a plugin message on `bsp_core:proxy` through the sender's connection: version, kind `chat`, the sender's UUID and name, the final text, the decorated `<Name> text` line as JSON, and this server's id. Team chat and chat a mod blocked are cancelled events and never go. `BspProxy.relayChat` lets another mod hand the relay its own public lines. The byte layout is in `docs/API.md`. One game test.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-10-18

### Added
- **A totem placed by a logout comes back when its owner returns.** A client crash is a logout, so the carried totem was placed where the player stood and seemed to vanish from the inventory. The placement is now remembered on the player: on their next login to this server, if the totem still stands where the logout put it, with the same identity, still theirs and not being stolen, it comes straight back into their inventory ("Your Shatter Totem is back with you. It stood at ... while you were away"). A totem mid-steal stays, with a warning; one that was taken is reported gone. While the player is away the totem stands in the world as before, so nothing is hidden and nothing is safer than it was. `restrictions.logoutReturnMinutes` (0 = however long ago) limits how old a placement may be to come back. Placements made by a death are not returned.
- **Plasma between worlds, part 1: the blocks and their forming.** Four new blocks in the plasma tab: Accelerator Intake and Accelerator Frame (seven frames round an intake, standing up with the middle empty, form a Plasma Accelerator; the intake faces the player who placed it and the ring stands across that facing), Decelerator Core and Decelerator Port (a port directly behind a core forms a Plasma Decelerator). Formed parts carry `formed` and know their controller; breaking any part un-forms the structure at once, a rebuilt one re-forms within a second. Placeholder cube looks from `tools/gen_link_assets.py`; the link cards, the hop, RF, the screen and the formed look follow in the next parts. Two game tests.
- **Plasma between worlds, part 2: Link Cards and Link Expanders.** A blank Link Card right-clicked on an Accelerator Intake is written with that accelerator's world and position (anyone may while nothing has fed the intake; after that the feeding totem's users and admins). Right-clicked on a Decelerator Core the written card goes into the core and links it to that accelerator, in any world of this server, loading the far chunk for a moment to check; the core says why when it cannot (no free link, no intake there, a blank card, a world on `plasma.linkDimensionBlacklist`). Sneak and right-click a core empty-handed to take the card out; breaking the core drops it and frees the link. An accelerator holds `plasma.acceleratorLinks` (1) links; each Link Expander right-clicked onto the intake adds one, `plasma.linkExpanderMax` (2) at most; sneak and right-click the intake empty-handed to take one out (not while its link is in use); breaking the intake drops them. Right-clicking either block empty-handed reports its state in a line until the screens come. One game test.
- **Plasma between worlds, part 3: the hop.** A formed Accelerator Intake is a plasma receiver like a Tank Port: any run reaching it feeds it (a face's worth each, `plasma.acceleratorMax` 3,000 mB/t in all) into a buffer that keeps the powers the plasma carries, the feeding totem's access list and its Anchor totem. Once a second the intake sends what it holds through its links in equal shares to every Decelerator that can take plasma this second (formed, with RF, with room), a link that cannot leaving its share to the others; the plasma arrives in the Decelerator Core's buffer with everything it carried, less `plasma.linkLoss` (0). Both ends burn RF: `plasma.acceleratorRfPerTick` (1,000) at the intake while any link delivers, `plasma.deceleratorRfPerTick` (100) at a core while it receives, from `plasma.linkRfCapacity` (200,000) pools fed through any Accelerator Frame or the intake, and any face of a core. A far side whose chunk is not ticking sleeps; every `plasma.linkWakeSeconds` (60) the accelerator loads it for five seconds and delivers, so a projector there can relight its own chunk loading. The empty-hand status lines now give the rates and the RF. Three game tests (delivery into the Nether with Overclock and the anchor totem, equal shares and a dead link, waking a sleeping far side).
- The Anchor totem a Projector follows is now a world position: a totem in the Overworld can keep a Nether projector's chunks loaded through a link. Projector Bases and Projectors saved before this change forget their totem once and pick it up again at the next feed.
- **Plasma between worlds, part 4: the Decelerator Port sends it on.** The port is a source for the cable runs on its free faces, like a Tank Port giving out of its tank: once a second it pushes what the core received (up to `plasma.acceleratorMax`, a face's worth per run) to the bases, chargers, injectors, tanks and accelerators those runs reach, with the powers the plasma carries, the feeding totem's access list and its Anchor totem. A Projector in the Nether fed this way keeps its chunks loaded under an Overworld totem's Anchor. Cables join an Accelerator Intake and a Decelerator Port on any face. The core's status line adds "sent on". One game test (an Overworld totem's Anchor II reaches a Nether projector, which the chunk ledger lists under that totem with its own chunk).
- **Plasma between worlds, part 5: the Worlds board and Jade.** Right-clicking an Accelerator Intake empty-handed opens its screen: a column for every world in play, the accelerator as a gold pip in its own column, every link as a pip in the column of its world with a line from the accelerator, lit while plasma passes, grey and dashed while the link sleeps; under each pip its position and state (delivering with its mB/t, asleep, no RF there, no port behind that core, full, gone), the in and out rates and the RF pool on the status line, the ring's state and the links held top right, the powers the plasma carries at the foot. Click a link to select it: UNLINK takes its card out at the far end (it drops beside the core); WAKE loads every sleeping far side for a moment. Both need the same access as writing a card. The server refreshes the board once a second. Jade: the intake (links, in and out, RF, or what is missing from the ring), a frame (part of a ring or loose), a core (its accelerator, arriving and sent on, RF, or no card, no port), a port (sending, nothing to send, no core). One game test (the view packet round trip and UNLINK from the accelerator's side).
- **Plasma between worlds, part 6: the look.** A formed ring stops being eight cubes: the blocks turn invisible and the intake draws the Octagon Synchrotron, a thick hull octagon with trim rings on both faces, a copper track inside it, eight coil heads with copper collars pointing at the centre, the intake's lit window on the bottom segment, and in the hole a black sphere in a slowly turning brass dodecahedron. Nothing moves without RF. With RF the heads light in a chase round the ring and the cage begins to turn; while a link delivers, charge pulses race round the track, arcs jump from the heads to the cage, a blue icosahedron turns inside the brass one and the accretion disc spins. The intake gives light 3 with RF and 7 while delivering, hums every three seconds with RF (the beacon's tone) with the portal's murmur on top while delivering, and portal particles drift in the hole. A formed decelerator draws an octagonal barrel of hull staves with three copper hoops round the small black hole in its cage, and behind it the port with a steel band and portholes that glow while plasma leaves; the hoops warm with RF, the halo lights while plasma arrives, and particles drift from the core.
- **Plasma between worlds, part 7: recipes, JEI, EMI and the guide.** Recipes: Accelerator Frame x2 (Tetrium Plates, Illyrium Ingots, a Machine Chassis), Accelerator Intake (two Charged Resonance Crystals, a Nether Star, two Illyrium Processors, a Charged Illyrium cable, plates, a chassis), Decelerator Core (two crystals, an Eye of Ender, Illyrium Ingots, Illyrium Tank Glass, plates, a chassis), Decelerator Port (plates, four Charged Illyrium cables, a chassis), Link Card (Illyrium Nuggets, an Ender Pearl, paper, a Basic Control Circuit), Link Expander (two Illyrium Ingots and two Illyrium Processors round an Eye of Ender; the first cut had four of each and cost more than the intake). JEI and EMI information pages for the accelerator's four things and the decelerator's two. Guide book entry "Plasma Across Worlds" (the ring, the far end, linking, RF and powers, sleeping and waking, the Worlds board) with the six recipes. `docs/BALANCE.md` rerun.

Every part of an assembled multiblock reads as the machine itself, and a working Decoy Totem reads as a Shatter Totem to everyone but its owner, so Jade cannot give a decoy away.

### Changed
- Totem Generators no longer load chunks every second while following their cables. Each second they look only at loaded chunks; every `projector.cableCheckSeconds` (default 30, and once when the generator loads) they follow the run all the way, loading the chunks along it for a moment. A run through chunks nobody is in is trusted between checks, so a far-off projector keeps its signal, and its chunk loading, without the cable's chunks being picked.

- **Coin Vault.** A safe block that holds only Shatter Coins, 27 slots per block. Vault blocks of the same owner that touch join into one vault of up to 3 x 3 x 3, paged one block at a time. Tabbed screen: Storage, Access (up to 8 online-added players who may open it) and Security (lock levels 1 to 3 paid in Gold, Diamond and Netherite coins; an Alarm paid in Gold coins that warns everyone with access). - **Vault lockpicking.** A player without access can pick the lock: 2 minutes, plus 1 minute per lock level, staying within 4 blocks. Success takes 25% of the coins, chosen at random; Illyrium coins are left alone while the vault holds 3 or fewer.
- **Vault interest.** Worked out on all of a player's vaults together, building up offline too, up to a 3 day cap, and redeemed at a vault by its owner. Pays XP levels and Tetrium Ingots for every 50 coin value stored (`vault.interest.coinValuePerUnit`), and up to 2 Illyrium Ingots per 3 days while any Netherite or Illyrium coin is stored.
- **Vaults cannot wall in a totem.** Anyone but the owner mines a vault block in a fixed 30 seconds with any tool and gets no coins: they are kept for the owner, who collects them by opening any of their vaults.
- A player may own at most 27 vault blocks (`vault.maxBlocksPerPlayer`). The owner's vault block keeps its lock and Alarm upgrades when broken and placed again.

- **Score Screen.** A thin display block. Panels placed side by side and above each other, facing the same way, join into one screen of up to 8 wide by 6 high. - **Scoring and leaderboard.** Every totem scores points by its tier (I to V: 1, 2, 4, 7, 10; `scoring.tierPoints`), and a player's score is the total for all their totems. Placed totems count for their owner, carried totems for whoever carries them. The board is rebuilt every 30 seconds (`scoring.refreshSeconds`), and within a second of a change on a single server, and sent to all clients. With MySQL storage on, every server publishes its rows and reads the combined network board. `/bsp score top` lists the top ten. The guide book has a "Scores and the Leaderboard" entry.
- **Optional MySQL storage for networks.** BSP-Core still keeps all its records in the world, so a single server needs no setup. With `storage.mode = "mysql"` it also connects to a MySQL or MariaDB database that you provide (host, port, database, user and password in the config) and creates its own tables there. Shared across the network: one first-join totem per player, the factory slice limit, and a record of every placed totem and factory slice by server. New operator commands: `/bsp storage status`, `/bsp storage test`, `/bsp storage migrate [force]`. Migrating copies a server's local records into the database, merges with what other servers already put there, and leaves the local records untouched as a backup. If the database cannot be reached, the server carries on from its local records and says so in the log. The slice limit is re-checked against the database every time a controller is placed; a slice that turns out to be over the network limit is removed and handed back.
- The MySQL driver is bundled inside the mod jar. `build/libs/bsp-core-<version>.jar` is the one to release; the `-slim` jar is the same mod without the driver.
