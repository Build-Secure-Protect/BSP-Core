Tracking Looks and Popout

Four looks to pick: the green outline on tagged machines while you hold the tagger, the style of the machine boxes, the popout window for a second monitor, and the box pinned to the game screen.

1. The green outline

Two machines are tagged: the factory on the left and the reactor on the right. A stone wall stands between you and them. The outline shows through walls within 48 blocks while the tagger is in your hand, and only for tags in the world you are in. Drag to turn the view.

A. Wire Box

A bright green line along every edge of the block, with a fainter second line just outside it for a soft glow. It is the thinnest look and hides the least of the machine.

B. Glow Shell

The same edges, plus a see-through green shell around the block that slowly breathes brighter and dimmer. It is the easiest look to spot from far away and behind walls.

C. Brackets and Name

Green brackets on the eight corners only, plus the machine's own name and its distance floating above it. It tells you which machine is which without opening the tagger.

2. The machine boxes

The same three machines at three sizes: a wide box, a small square, and a short strip. A box reads the machine as often as its own screen would (on the same server). Use "In transmitter range" to see the OPERATE badge, which marks a box you can click to open the machine's own screen.

A. Holo Cards

The Holo HUD look of BSP-Core: dark glass, turquoise, gold for energy. Each machine gets its own drawing: one bar per process for a factory, a temperature dial and four tanks for the reactor, and item slots for anything else. Each drawing re-arranges itself to fit its box.

B. Mod Look

Small copies of each mod's own grey screen: the same slot frames, bar colours and words (Status, Temp, Burn) that players know from Mekanism and the rest. While you are in range, the reactor's SCRAM key shows.

C. Gauges Only

Numbers first: one big reading per machine (average progress, temperature, items stored), a line of detail under it, and thin bars when the box has room. It is the lightest to draw and the easiest to read from across a room on a second monitor.

3. The popout window

The popout is a second window you can put on another monitor. Drag the corner of each window below, or use the size keys, to see it fit any shape. Inside 2:1 of the page's own shape the grid stretches. Past that, the boxes re-stack in reading order, in a column for a tall window or a row for a wide one. Click FOCUS, the lamp or "Press F8" to see the focused state, the one the focus key gives, where the pause menu stays shut (F8 is only an example: the key starts unbound). Page tabs are clickable in every state.

A. Tab Strip

The page tabs sit in a thin strip under the window's title bar, with FOCUS on the right. Focused, the window gets a turquoise border, and the map box gains JourneyMap's toolbar so you can drag and zoom it.

B. Side Rail

The pages are numbered keys in a narrow rail down the left edge, so the grid keeps the whole height. A lamp at the foot of the rail shows focus.

C. Bottom Dock

Round page keys in a dock along the bottom, with the page name and a hint: "Press F8 to use", or while focused "In use · F8 or Esc to go back". The grid keeps the full width.

4. The pinned box in game

One box from any page, pinned to a corner of the game screen at small or medium size. The keys in the middle exist only in this mock-up, so you can try the sizes and corners.

A. Framed

The box in a full turquoise frame on dark glass, the same as in the Holo Grid.

B. Tabbed

A small turquoise tab on top says which page and box it is (PAGE 1 · 3/5), over a box with a dim edge.

C. Glass

No frame: the readings float on light, see-through glass with one turquoise line along the top. It covers the least of the world.

Cost on the graphics card

PartWhat it costs
PopoutA second window that shares the game's graphics. The grid is drawn into an off-screen picture at the popout's size, at 30 or 60 frames a second as you choose, then copied to the window once. It is redrawn only when a reading changes or a bar moves, and otherwise the last picture is shown again. Most of the time it costs one copy.
Map boxThe map's tiles are kept in memory and drawn only when the map moves or changes.
Item iconsDrawn once into a cache per item and reused, so a box full of slots costs about the same as a flat picture.
OutlineA handful of lines per tagged machine, drawn only while the tagger is in your hand.
