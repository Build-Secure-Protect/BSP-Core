Lead-Lined Chest Looks

Radioactive items in an ordinary chest keep radiating. Anything in a lead-lined chest never does. The chest joins a neighbour into a double chest, like a vanilla chest, and its recipe is eight lead plates around a chest. Alongside it come three radiation shielding pieces for building a shielded room: a panel that stops all radiation, a window that lets 10% through and a door. This page shows three looks for the chest, three for the shielding set and three for the chest's screen. Pick one of each, or mix parts.

Drag a view to turn it. A stone block stands beside each one for scale.

Built withBlock models from a generator script, like the other BSP blocks. Minecraft models are boxes, so the round parts here (the padlock, the cask's edges, the gauge, the porthole, the wheel) are drawn with few sides and will look stepped in game. The lid opens through a block-entity renderer, as a vanilla chest's does. The window is drawn with the translucent render type so the glass shows through.

5. Pick, with defaults

  1. The chest. The default is A, Lead Strongbox, because it still reads as a chest from across a room, and the yellow plate says what it is for. With B, the chest looks like reactor equipment and suits a reactor hall. With C, it looks like lab furniture and suits Nuclear Science bases.
  2. The shielding set. The default is A, Riveted Lead Sheet, so the wall and the chest look made of the same metal. With B, a shielded room looks like a clean lab. With C, it looks like a bunker, and the vault door reads as the strongest door in the base, which it isn't (it is as hard as iron).
  3. The screen. The default is B, Lead panel: players know at once that it is a chest, and the tag tells them it is shielded. With A, it is the vanilla screen with nothing added. With C, it matches BSP-Core's other screens and states the rule in words.
