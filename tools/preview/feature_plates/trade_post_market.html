Trade Post Concepts

Three designs each for the Trade Post block, its main screen (the market and the list of players at a Trade Post), the trade screen, and the Market page on the Score Screen. The screens are drawn at Minecraft GUI size in the Cash Machine skin the Bank and the shops use. Names, items and prices are made up for the picture. Nothing is built.

Picked (owner, 2026-10-27)

Main screen C, trade screen C, Market page B. The block: none of these three; round 2 has new looks with scales on a counter, the screen behind and labelled buttons.

Decided (owner, 2026-10-27)

  • Live trades with anyone at a Trade Post on the network, other servers included. A request lasts 30 s, a player trading shows as busy.
  • 12 slots a side, a pieces box under each, READY under that. Any change turns both READY lights off; the 5 s countdown locks the slots.
  • Space is worked out at the end; if it does not fit nothing moves and both players are told who is short. Leaving cancels.
  • Market lots of up to 9 stacks, 10 a player, 7 days. The seller pays 5% when listing, the buyer pays the price plus 5%; the tax goes into the lottery pot.
  • Totems, Forged totems and Totem Cores are refused. Admins place the posts. Search and sort above the lots. A MARKET key on the Portable Banker behind its own unlock.

1. The block

Two blocks wide, two tall, one deep, turned to face the player who places it. TRADE on the screen. The grey cube is one block, for scale. Drag to turn.

A. Street Kiosk

The Cash Machine made twice as wide: cream casing, one wide green screen, a slanted row of beige keys, and two item hatches at the foot with arrows between them. Reads as the screens look.

B. Exchange Pillar

A wide cousin of the Bank's Vault Pillar: dark steel, riveted bands, the screen under a hood, amber keys down both sides, a beacon on each top corner, and a drawer that slides from one side to the other while a trade runs.

C. Market Booth

A wooden booth with a striped awning, the screen set into the back panel, a brass counter with two trays and a lantern on each front post. Ties it to the Market Stalls.

2. The main screen

Opens on the market. The players at a Trade Post anywhere on the network are listed at the right; picking a name lights the trade key. The same market screen opens from the Portable Banker's MARKET key, without the player list.

A. One screen, keys below

One wide phosphor screen split in two: the lots on the left, the players on the right. A strip of physical keys underneath, grouped as market keys, the trade key, and CLOSE, with gaps between the groups.

B. Key column, like the Bank

The Bank's layout with a second, narrow screen: the keys in a column on the left, the market in the middle, the players on their own screen at the right with the ASK TO TRADE key under it. The widest of the three.

C. Touch screen with tabs

No physical keys: one tall screen with MARKET, SELL and COLLECT tabs along the top, the lots as cards with big item pictures, and the players in a darker band at the right. Buttons are drawn on the screen.

3. The trade screen

Your offer on the left, theirs on the right, a pieces box and READY under each, the chat at the bottom, your inventory below or beside it.

A. Mirror

The two offers face each other with a small screen between them that tells you what is happening: who is ready, the countdown as a big number, or who is short of space. The chat screen runs the full width above the inventory.

B. Ready lamps in the middle

The middle column holds two large lamps, one per player, and the one READY key. The countdown is a bar across the whole screen that drains over 5 seconds. Messages show in the chat screen as lines from the Trade Post.

C. Low and wide

The inventory and the chat sit side by side at the bottom, so the screen is wider but much shorter and fits small windows. The countdown is five pips that go out one a second.

4. The Market page on the Score Screen

A new "Market" board in the Score Screen's settings, shown alone or in the rotation. Each page below is a 4 by 2 screen in one of the three looks every board already has. It scrolls slowly through every lot.

A. Ledger rows

One lot a row: the item picture, its count and name, "+2 more" for a lot of several stacks, how long ago it was listed, and the price in gold at the right.

B. Tiles

A big item picture per lot with the price under it and the name and age in small type; four across on this size of screen, more on a wider one.

C. Beside the leaderboard

The Side look: the leaderboard keeps the left two thirds, the market scrolls in a column at the right.
