Totem Looks

A totem owner with VIP gets a LOOKS tab in the totem screen to dress every totem they own: a body colour or gradient, painted pixels, their own head, and a hat. The player at the top of the score board gets a glow and a stream of sparks on their totems. Use the panel to try a look; every screen and view on the page follows it. The totem is the real Guardian model (kneeling, head bowed, orb in hand). The numbers on the screens are made-up examples.

1. The LOOKS tab

A new tab in the totem screen, after CHUNKS and before TEAM, for the owner while they have VIP. One look covers all their totems. Each screen below is the full 326 by 293 totem screen at GUI size; the radiation strip along the bottom stays as it is today.

A. Dressing Room

Everything on one page. The turning preview on the left shows the look on your own totem, and SEE STEAL shows the red pulse a raid still gives it. On the right, BODY has SOLID, GRADIENT and PAINT, a colour square with a hue strip, and the gradient bar with its three stops. HEAD swaps the creeper face for your own head. HAT is a grid of every hat with its name under it.

SAVE puts the look on all your totems at once; RESET goes back to plain gold. PAINT opens the paint bench (layout C) as a page of its own.

B. Three Pages

A bigger preview, less at once. BODY, HEAD and HAT are small pages inside the tab. BODY (shown) has a colour wheel, a brightness bar and eight saved colours, then the gradient with up to three stops that runs top to base or side to side, and PAINT PIXELS to open the paint bench.

HEAD is two large cards (totem face, your head). HAT is a list with names, and the preview zooms on the head while you pick.

C. Paint Bench

The painter comes first. Pick a body part and paint its unfolded faces pixel by pixel, at the totem's own size (one texture pixel is one model pixel, 1,552 in all), with 16 colours, PEN, FILL, PICK, UNDO and a mirror. START fills a part from your colour or gradient first, so nobody starts from a blank. The example is painted flames.

The small preview on the right turns with the paint. Head and hat are keys under it. The other two layouts reach this page through PAINT.

2. Hats and toppers

Thirteen to start: top hat, halo, flat cap, beer cap, sports cap, beanie, wizard hat, devil horns, cowboy hat, Viking helmet, party hat, chef's hat and propeller cap. Pick one in the panel. The three concepts are the same models, fitted three ways. Hats are separate models drawn by the renderer, never part of the block; round parts are low-poly meshes of 8 to 28 sides.

A. Snug fit

True to the totem's scale. The hat sits on the bowed head and tilts with it. Each hat keeps its own colours: a black top hat with a red band, a red beanie with a white bobble.

B. In your colours

The hat matches the body. The same models, but the main cloth takes your body colour and the trim a darker shade of it. Metal, glass, cans and gold stay as they are.

C. Big and bold

Cartoon size. About a third bigger, grown from the top of the head, so a hat reads from across a base. Own colours, as in A.

3. Score Leader glow

Only on the totems of the player at the top of the score board, and moves to the next leader as soon as the board changes. The glow is drawn full bright on the see-through entity layer, as the aura cubes are, so shader packs show it as light. The sparks are ordinary client particles with a client setting to turn them off. A cloaked totem is never sent to the client, so it never glows.

A. Gilded Aura

A thin gold shell breathes around the body, and gold sparks drift up from the plinth, about four a second. Quiet in the day, plain to see at night.

B. Crown of Light

A gold crown floats and turns above the head, above any hat. Small stars circle the body and a soft gold pool lies on the ground under the plinth.

C. Victory Pillar

The plinth rim and the lamp studs light gold, a faint gold column rises three blocks, and sparks spiral up inside it. The easiest of the three to spot from far away.
