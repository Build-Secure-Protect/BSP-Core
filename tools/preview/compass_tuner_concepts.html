BSP-Core · mock-ups for the owner's pick

Compass Tuner

The tuner keeps the compass upgrades, so losing a compass no longer loses the coins spent on it. Players buy Across, Up or down, Cooldown and Tune speed on the tuner at today's compass prices. A compass put in it is tuned to the tuner's levels over time. Each tune uses lapis, nether quartz and redstone, with FE, and Wave Plasma speeds it up. Everything below uses made-up example levels.

1. The block

One block each. Drag a view to turn it. Each model has a hollow or recess where the compass sits, round brass and copper parts, three feeds (lapis, quartz and redstone), a Wave Plasma inlet and an FE socket. The little screen on each block counts down the tune.

A. Gimbal Cradle · picked

A slate plinth with a brass gimbal. The compass lies on a pin in the middle of two brass rings that turn around it while it tunes. The rings sit over a round sunken well. Three corner posts carry open hoppers filled with lapis, quartz and redstone, and copper pipes run from them into the well. The fourth post is a glass bulb that glows blue with plasma. A recessed front panel holds the countdown screen and a labelled TUNE plate. The FE socket is at the back, and a rolled chart with a red ribbon lies on the plinth.

B. Tuning Fork Cabinet

An iron cabinet with an open front bay. The compass turns on a brass turntable inside, lit turquoise from the bay's ceiling. A tuning fork stands on the roof and hums (its prongs shiver) while it works. Three glass vials on the left wall show how much lapis, quartz and redstone is left. The plasma pipe comes in on the right through a flanged window. The top band holds the countdown, a round dial gauge and a TUNE plate. It stands on four round feet, with a leather notebook on the roof.

C. Lodestone Coil Desk

A slate desk with a copper coil tower. The compass floats above a wound copper coil and spins while it tunes. A glass plasma tube runs up the back into the coil. The desk has a sunken tray at the front with three round cups of lapis, quartz and redstone. The tower's recessed face holds the countdown screen, a TUNE plate and a round red key. The FE socket is a red ring on the right side, and a notebook and pencil lie in the tray.

2. The tuner screen

Each layout shows the same things: the compass slot, the three material slots with the amount a tune needs, FE and plasma, the time left and the clock time it should be ready, the four upgrade keys with their prices, and what the compass will get. The keys that tune a compass (Across, Up or down, Cooldown) are grouped apart from the tuner's own Tune speed key. Switch the example to see the other states.

A. Ladders and Bay

The upgrades are listed on the left, each with its level pips, the reach or time it gives now and next, the price and a key. The bay is on the right: the compass in a ring that fills as it tunes, the three materials under it, then FE and plasma. Everything fits on one page, so it is the closest to today's compass screen.

B. Two Tabs

The TUNE tab reads left to right: compass in, a progress arrow with the time, then the compass it will become. Under that it says what changes (100 to 900 across, for example), and the materials sit in a row below. The UPGRADES tab reuses the compass's REACH side view, so players see the box grow when they buy WIDER or DEEPER.

C. Dial · picked, with the reach view from B

A big dial is the tuner's face. The compass sits in the middle, the progress runs round the rim like a clock hand, and the three materials sit on the dial at 8, 4 and 6 o'clock. Under the dial is B's REACH view: the turquoise box is the tuner's reach and the dotted gold box the next Across level. The upgrades are compact cards in the right column, with FE and plasma under them. The TUNE key sits at the bottom, apart from the upgrade keys, and is lit only when a compass, the materials and FE are in.

3. The compass's own screen after the change

The coin tiles, START and the REACH view stay the same. The cooldown key and the WIDER and DEEPER keys go, and a line says where the levels came from. An untuned compass still tracks, at the base reach (100 across, 10 up or down) with the 10-minute cooldown.
